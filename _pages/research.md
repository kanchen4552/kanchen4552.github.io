My research develops statistical and machine learning methods for reliable causal reasoning in complex real-world data. A central theme is to build methods that are theoretically principled, interpretable, robust to bias and distributional shifts, and useful for scientific and clinical decision-making.

Methodologically, my work spans causal inference, causal machine learning and AI, high-dimensional statistics, and large-scale biomedical data analysis. I am particularly interested in addressing unmeasured confounding, selection bias, residual confounding, interpretability, robustness, and generalizability.

Substantively, my research is motivated by applications in clinical trials, oncology, genomics and epigenomics, patient-centric health research, and policy evaluation. The long-term goal is to develop reliable causal AI tools that improve human health, support scientific discovery, and inform evidence-based policy.
